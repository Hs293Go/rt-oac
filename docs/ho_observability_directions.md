## 1. When higher-order observability genuinely matters

All four lenses converge on the **same object** — the observation codistribution flag $d\Omega^{(r)}=\mathrm{span}\{dL_{\mathbf f}^k h:0\le k\le r\}$ — and the **same single eigenvalue law** for the worst-observable direction:

$$\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf W^{(r)}) \;=\; C(\mathbf x,\mathbf u)\,\Delta T^{\,2r_*+1}, \qquad C\propto \sigma_{\min}^2(\mathcal O^{(r_*)}),\quad r\ge r_*.$$

Three numbers govern everything: **$r_*$** (the local observability index = chain depth = the only order that matters), **$\Delta T^{2r_*+1}$** (the analytic weight), and **$C$** (the information content, set by the measurement set, *not* by order). The STLOG *is* the per-stage Fisher information; $\mathrm{CRLB}=(\mathbf W^{(r)})^{-1}$. So "does order help" is literally "does adding a Lie row shrink the worst CRLB direction by more than the noise floor."

**Higher-order observability adds real, usable information iff ALL THREE hold:**

- **(i) Geometric — there is a chain to climb.** The target direction is first revealed at some $k\ge2$: $\mathbf v\in\mathcal H_{k-1}\setminus\mathcal H_k$, i.e. $dL_{\mathbf f}^i h\,\mathbf v=0$ for $i<k$ and $\ne0$ at $k$. This requires a genuine drift-induced integrator chain of depth $r_*\ge2$ behind a **low-dimensional** output: $s(r_*+1)\ge\dim\mathcal Z$ with $s$ small. The driver is the **drift $\mathbf f$**: each $L_{\mathbf f}$ propagates the differential one step backward along $\mathbf f$'s coupling graph, so a state buried $d$ links deep surfaces only at order $d$.

- **(ii) Analytic — the weight is not floored.** $\Delta T^{2r_*+1}$ must clear the noise floor: $C\,\Delta T^{2r_*+1}\gtrsim\sigma_{\text{meas}}^2$. This needs **fast / strongly nonlinear dynamics** (large $L_{\mathbf f}^{r_*}h$ curvature) and/or a **horizon comparable to the buried-state timescale** (weak fast/slow separation). The exponent is steep: at $r_*=5,\ \Delta T=0.2$, the weight is $\sim2\times10^{-8}$; at $r_*=2$ it is $\Delta T^5\sim3\times10^{-4}$. **A shallow chain is survivable; a deep chain is self-defeating unless the rate or horizon is large.**

- **(iii) Informational — the data is physically there.** $\sigma_{\min}(\mathcal O^{(r_*)})>0$ with margin: the output must be rich enough that the buried mode is actually excited. Order changes the **exponent**; only the measurement set changes the **coefficient** $C$.

**The two sharp boundaries (and the redundancy zone):**

1. **Rank-saturation / order-redundancy boundary (fails i).** For $k>r_*$, $dL_{\mathbf f}^k h\in d\Omega^{(r_*)}$ *identically* — extra rows are span-redundant and cannot raise rank. The benefit of "order" is **entirely the benefit of reaching $r_*$**, never of stacking past it ($r>r_*$ contributes only $O(\Delta T^{2r_*+2})$, the leading exponent frozen at $r_*$). This is the **single most important and most-violated fact in the literature**: "higher order" is almost always "reach a modest $r_*$ (often 2–3)," not an open-ended climb. The flat-output O0 model ($\dot{\mathbf x}=\mathbf u$, velocity a *droppable input* not a state) is the extreme — $r_*$ is met at order 1, and orders 1→5 are pure redundancy (rt-oac §6.7).

2. **dt-suppression boundary (fails ii).** A row that is linearly independent in the *codistribution* is numerically null in the *Gramian* when $\Delta T^{2r_*+1}$ is floored. Geometric rank $\ne$ usable information. **Fix: lengthen the horizon (the steep knob), never raise $r$.**

3. **Information-limit boundary (fails iii).** The coefficient is capped by the measurement Jacobian's conditioning: $\lambda_{\min}\le\sigma_{\min}^2(\mathcal O^{(r_*)})\cdot\beta\,\Delta T^{2r_*+1}$. **One scalar range gives one row per step; no order manufactures rank from an under-determined output.** Fix: a richer $h$ (2nd anchor) — which *lowers* $r_*$ AND *raises* $\sigma_{\min}$, helping twice — or accumulated motion.

**The closed-loop fourth condition (the one our O1 loop violated):**

- **(iv) Dual-control safe.** Closing the loop on the HO direction must not **outrun the filter**. The excitation that lights up the high-$r_*$ mode must keep the *carried* estimate consistent. This is not in the open-loop theory at all, and it is exactly where the full-fidelity range-only quad died: optimizing HO observability *harder* drove estimator-destabilizing maneuvers (non-monotonic over-excitation → carried-estimate divergence).

**Why range-only formation flight was a poor showcase.** It violates the conditions in *both* of its model variants — which is precisely what makes it diagnostic:
- **O0 (flat kinematic):** velocity is a droppable input, $r_*$ is met at order 1 → fails (i) (order-redundancy); the weakly-observable tangential mode is one-scalar-range starved → fails (iii) (information-limit). Resolved only by accumulated motion or a 2nd anchor — never by Lie order.
- **O1 (full relative-pose quad):** the chain is genuine ($r_*\ge5$, pos←vel←accel←attitude), so (i) holds — but $\Delta T^{11}\sim10^{-8}$ → fails (ii) (dt-suppressed at the real rate), and the only regime where the structural advantage pays is the full range-only carried estimator, which fails (iv) (the documented divergence NO-GO).
- **The lean-estimator killer (iii/i collapse):** with an IMU/AHRS filter that *directly measures* velocity and attitude, $r_*$ collapses to $\sim1$, O1's structural advantage is moot, and the simpler O0 objective **dominates** (95% bounded vs O1's 75% peak).

The lesson: range-only cooperative navigation sits at the **intersection of three of the four failure modes**. It is not that HO observability is weak — it is that this problem is measurement-poor *and* dt-suppressed *and* (in the only regime where the chain is real) dual-control-unsafe. **The fix is a different problem, not a different metric.**

## 2. Where it shines — scenario classes

Sixteen scenarios were adversarially scored; **none earned an unqualified "yes,"** and that is the honest headline. The ceiling was 7/10. Every strong candidate clears the two traps that killed range-only (geometric + dt) and then stumbles on one or both of the *same* two closing arguments: **richer-sensor dominance** (a cheap direct measurement collapses $r_*$, reproducing the lean-estimator result) and **carried-loop dual-control hazard** (exciting the weakly-observable mode destabilizes the filter). Grouped by mechanism:

### Class A — Buried scalar-parameter / chemical-kinetics (strongest: CSTR, #1 7/10, #5 6.5/10)

- **State/measurement:** reactant concentration $C_A$ (+ unknown activation energy / fouling) from a **single thermocouple** (temperature only).
- **HO mechanism:** $C_A\to k(T)C_A\to$ heat release $\to dT/dt$. Concentration has relative degree $\ge2$; the unknown parameter appears only at 3rd order. The strongly nonlinear Arrhenius coupling $k(T)=A e^{-E/RT}$ makes each $L_{\mathbf f}h$ pull in large curvature ($d\ln k/dT\sim0.07$–$0.09$/K near ignition).
- **Why it clears the traps the quad failed:** $\dim\mathcal Z\sim2$–3, so $r_*\sim2$–3 (exponent 5–7, **not** 11), and CSTR control samples at $\Delta T\sim1$–10 s, so $\Delta T^{2r_*+1}$ is $O(1)$ to $10^7$ — **off the floor by 8–15 orders vs the quad's $2\times10^{-8}$.** This is the mirror image of the range-only corner: *shallow chain + large $\Delta T$ + strong nonlinearity* = the theory's sweet spot. The information is small-dimensional and genuinely encoded ($\sigma_{\min}$ bounded away from zero and **controllable** — OAC steers onto the high-$dk/dT$ locus).
- **Excitation:** steer hot, into the steep Arrhenius regime, to maximize $\sigma_{\min}(\mathcal O^{(r_*)})$.
- **Dodges carried-divergence?** **No — and worse than the quad.** The excitation that maximizes observability pushes toward the ignition/runaway manifold, where the dynamics are most positive-feedback-unstable and the EKF linearization least valid, on a single scalar leash. This is the dual-control NO-GO with a *thermal-runaway* consequence. **Verdict: the cleanest open-loop HO win in the whole battery; closing the loop requires a safety-certified MPC backstop gating the excitation.** Also capped by sensor dominance: an inline composition/calorimetry/NIR sensor collapses $r_*\to1$.

### Class B — Inertial / scale / bias buried behind integrator chains (in-flight alignment #2, monocular VIO scale #3/#8/#12, IMU self-calibration #10)

- **State/measurement:** gyro bias + heading from velocity-match innovation (#2); metric scale + gravity-tilt from bearing-only + IMU (#3/#8/#12); scale-factor/misalignment from the rate/specific-force product (#10).
- **HO mechanism:** the canonical INS / VINS chains. Heading/bias enter the velocity innovation only after the specific-force cross product $\mathbf f\times\boldsymbol\phi$ propagates tilt (order 2, the Goshen-Meskin/Bar-Itzhack PWCS result). Scale is projection-invariant and surfaces only at the *2nd* output Lie derivative where specific force is double-integrated (Martinelli VI-SfM); Fisher info for scale $\propto\|\dot{\mathbf v}_W\|^2$ — **manufacturable by motion** (figure-eight 4.8% vs straight-line 9.2% scale error).
- **Why it's favorable:** measurement is **rich** ($s=3$ velocity-match, or many bearings/frame + 6-axis IMU — not one scalar range), so the mode is information-supported, not starved. $r_*$ is **moderate** (2–3), so the weight is $\Delta T^5$, not $\Delta T^{11}$, and agile/fast dynamics lift it off the floor. For #10, the killer feature: the parameter-to-output Jacobian is *literally proportional to the input* ($M_g\omega$, $M_a f$), so $C\propto|\omega|^2,|f|^2$ — **OAC commands the exact quantity that defeats dt-suppression.** This is the rare case where the controller owns the information coefficient.
- **Excitation:** maneuver (specific-force variation, curvature/jerk, sign-reversing coning).
- **Two recurring deflations:**
  - **The HO benefit is "reach $r_*=2$," not "go high-order."** Orders 3–5 are frozen-exponent redundant. A competent 2nd-order UKF/EKF already captures this implicitly. The marginal value of HO-STLOG-*as-objective* over a 2nd-order filter on a well-excited trajectory is the genuinely contested, often-overstated quantity.
  - **Richer-sensor dominance is canonical here.** A single direct heading sensor (magnetometer / dual-antenna GNSS) makes alignment full-rank at order 2 *without* maneuver; a single 1-D rangefinder/altimeter (Range-VIO) makes scale observable *without* excitation, collapsing $r_*$. **This is exactly the §6.12 lean-estimator pattern, and it is exactly the user's X-IO LIDAR+VIO port** — which supplies that very sensor. HO is the *second-best* tool, reached for only in the strict heading-denied / mono-cam-only corner.
  - **Carried-loop hazard (iv) unresolved.** An entire VINS subfield (OC-EKF, FEJ/FEJ2, invariant-EKF, T-ESKF) exists *because* linearized filters spuriously inject information along precisely the scale/gravity/yaw directions OAC would excite. Exciting near-unobservable modes on a carried estimate is structurally the same dual-control hazard. No candidate here supplies an FEJ/OC analog — it is asserted-safe, not shown-safe.

### Class C — Bilinear / sensorless electromechanical & power systems (induction motor #6, generator DSE #11)

- **State/measurement:** rotor speed/flux from stator current via the bilinear EMF chain (#6); generator rotor angle/flux from PMU frequency & RoCoF (#11).
- **HO mechanism:** speed never appears in the output map directly; it first enters via $L_{\mathbf f}$ of the flux dynamics (order $\ge2$, current←flux←speed·flux). At zero stator frequency the chain degenerates ($r_*\to\infty$) — **this is why injection is mandatory**, and it makes the paper's deepest lesson *literal*: **OAC's job is to lower/restore $r_*$** (injection moves $\infty\to2$).
- **Why favorable:** electrical $\tau\sim$ ms, so $\Delta T/\tau\sim O(1)$ and the order-2 weight is $\sim10^{-2}$–$10^{-1}$, **not** $10^{-8}$. Fast + strongly nonlinear (bilinear) — conditions (i)+(ii) hold precisely where the quad failed (ii).
- **The decisive deflation:** the field's *robust* solution at zero frequency is **high-frequency / saliency injection**, which manufactures a near-*algebraic* measurement of the flux angle — i.e. it **raises $s$ and collapses $r_*\to1$**, the measurement-enrichment escape, not the Lie-order escape. The candidate even smuggles "saturation-saliency" into its own HO justification. For #11, real PMUs report full V&I phasors making $E_q',E_d'$ near-algebraically observable; the buried structure is an *artifact of a deliberately impoverished output* no real deployment uses. Plus the closed-loop hazard: sensorless IM controllers are documented-unstable at zero frequency; the probe rings a stability-constrained live grid.

### Class D — Active-perception / field-seeking with operator-owned dither (source seeking #4, SfM-by-parallax #16)

- **State/measurement:** gradient AND Hessian of an unknown scalar field on a nonholonomic vehicle, single scalar sample (#4); inverse-depth from a single moving camera (#16).
- **HO mechanism:** gradient is invisible at order 0 and appears only via motion ($L_{\mathbf f}\phi=\nabla\phi\cdot\mathbf v$); Hessian at order 2. Extremum-seeking theory *proves* periodic dither is required to estimate the gradient — i.e. the hidden state genuinely lives in a higher Lie derivative.
- **The unique structural advantage:** the operator **owns the $\Delta T$ knob** — the effective excitation rate *is* the commanded dither frequency, so $\Delta T^{2r_*+1}$ is kept off the floor *by construction*. This is the rare legitimate escape from dt-suppression, and $r_*=2$ is the sweet spot (deep enough that order is required, shallow enough that $\Delta T^5$ survives). **This class is the cleanest closed-loop instance of the geometric+analytic conditions** because the controller, not the environment, sets the timescale.
- **Where it still stumbles:** the *gradient* is robust; the **Hessian** (the deep state the HO claim rests on) sits on the information-limit corner — one scalar projection $\mathbf v^\top H\mathbf v$ per instant resolves a multi-component $H$ only via accumulated motion + dither *geometry* (a Lissajous spanning enough $\mathbf v$-directions), not Lie order per se. And in noisy/intermittent fields, dithering harder near the source (where $\nabla\phi\to0$) is the over-excitation hazard. #16 is honestly an $r_*=2$ problem mislabeled as high-order: orders 3–5 are flat to 4 sig figs, and the usable knob is horizon/baseline; a second camera (stereo) collapses $r_*\to1$.

### Class E — Buried-state but excitation-self-refuting or sensor-dominated (aeroelastic flutter #7, LuGre friction #14, legged-robot wrench #15, quantum Hamiltonian #9)

These pass the geometric test (real chains: aero-lag states, bristle state $z$, external wrench, interior couplings) and several pass the dt test on physics, but each has a **specific structural defeater**:
- **#14 LuGre is self-refuting:** the prescribed excitation (presliding dither through $v=0$) is *exactly where $z$ is provably unobservable* ($\dot e_z=0$ when $w\equiv0$; $\det\mathcal O\approx0$ in quasi-static motion). The HO information lives in fast slip, the controller is told to dwell where $\sigma_{\min}\to0$. An information-limit collapse *at the operating point*.
- **#9 quantum:** the "fast rates ⇒ not dt-suppressed" claim is a rate/depth conflation. Suppression is **depth-driven** (factorial moment weight $1/((r_*!)^2(2r_*+1))$ × Lieb-Robinson exponential-in-distance signal), and rescaling to GHz cancels in dimensionless $J\Delta T$. Deep interior couplings — the entire motivation — are the suppressed ones. Plus a **hard decoherence horizon ceiling** you cannot integrate past (the quantum CRLB-floor NO-GO). The field's winning move is to *lower* $r_*$ via multiple probe placements, not stack commutator order.
- **#7/#15:** the single-sensor premise is fragile (aeroelastic rigs carry many channels; F/T sensors / momentum observers recover the wrench excitation-free), and the excitation drives toward an *unstable* regime (flutter LCO / loss of balance) — dual-control with a *physical-divergence* consequence.

**The unifying pattern across all 16:** the moment a candidate has a real, deep, dt-survivable chain, it *also* tends to have (a) a cheap direct sensor that collapses $r_*$, and (b) an excitation that points toward the system's least-stable manifold. The two are not coincidental — **deep chains behind low-D measurements are intrinsically the regimes where a direct sensor is most valuable and where carried estimation is most fragile.** This is the deep reason range-only was instructive, not anomalous.

## 3. How to leverage it in closed loop — a coherent recipe

The open-loop theory tells you *whether* HO information exists. The closed-loop problem is whether you can *spend control to acquire it without diverging the filter*. The six mechanism designs decompose into a single layered recipe:

**Layer 0 — Diagnose the regime online (Adaptive Lie-order & horizon).** Compute three cheap gauges from the already-formed Lie-row Gram, *after de-Hilberting each row by $\Delta T^k/k!$*:
- $g_{\text{rank}}$: incremental $\Delta\lambda_k$ from the $k$-th row → the **effective $\hat r_*$**. Saturates early → drop to $r=1$, lean objective.
- $g_{\text{dt}}=C\,\Delta T^{2\hat r_*+1}/\sigma^2$: SNR of the buried mode. $<1$ → grow $\Delta T$ (steep knob), **not** $r$.
- $g_{\text{info}}=\sigma_{\min}(\mathcal O^{(\hat r_*)})$: floored → flag for a richer measurement; no order or horizon helps.

This makes the §1 conditions *operational* and self-enforcing: it auto-selects $r=1$ in the O0/lean regime (reproducing "low-order dominates" with no hand-switch), grows the horizon under dt-suppression, and only engages HO when all three gauges are green.

**Layer 1 — Plan on the covariance, not the point estimate (belief-space / dual-control MPC).** This is the **primary fix for what broke O1.** Make $\Sigma$ the planning state; propagate the EKF Riccati with $\mathbf W^{(r)}$ as stage FIM; minimize a functional of the *predicted posterior* ($\log\det\Sigma_T$ or $\max_t\lambda_{\max}\Sigma_t$) instead of $\max\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf W^{(r)})$. **dt-suppression and information-limit become self-enforcing constraints**: a $10^{-8}$-weighted HO row produces negligible $\Sigma$-reduction, so the optimizer simply won't pay control effort/risk for it. A maneuver that would outrun the filter shows up as $\Sigma$ *growing*, not as free observability. This is explicit dual control, and it cures all four failure modes at once.

**Layer 2 — Throttle the excitation (observability dither).** Recast HO-OAC from "maximize and hope" to "excite exactly enough": $\min\|\mathbf u_{\text{dither}}\|$ s.t. $\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf W^{(r_*)})\ge\lambda^\star$, with $\mathbf u=\mathbf u_{\text{nom}}+\mathbf u_{\text{dither}}$. The throttle $\lambda^\star$ — set from the **estimator's consistency budget** (so predicted NEES stays in its $\chi^2$ band) — *is* the dual-control safeguard. When a lean filter collapses $r_*\to1$, the dither auto-shuts-off ($\epsilon\to0$), degenerating gracefully to the dominant low-order objective. Infeasibility of the minimal dither *is* the dt-suppression flag.

**Layer 3 — Co-design the metric and the filter so the estimator can metabolize HO information.**
- **Metric:** whiten by the moment weights ($\tilde{\mathbf W}=\mathbf S^{-1}\mathbf W^{(r)}\mathbf S^{-1}$, $\mathbf S=\mathrm{diag}(\Delta T^j/j!)$) to remove the $1/\Delta T^{2r_*+1}$ gradient blow-up that *is* the over-excitation driver; **project onto the HO-only subspace** $P_{HO}=\mathcal H_{r_*-1}\setminus\mathcal H_{r_*}$ so excitation isn't spent on already-observable low-order modes; and use the **empirical observability Gramian along the carried trajectory** (observability about the *filter's belief*, not about truth) as the divergence-safe surrogate.
- **Filter:** the EKF's single-point Jacobian discards exactly the $r_*$-th-derivative curvature that carries HO content — HO-information-seeking control and first-order filtering are at cross-purposes. Replace/augment with a **sigma-point/cubature filter** (captures 2nd+ moments, the natural first rung), an **observability-constrained filter** (projects updates onto the consistent observable subspace, killing spurious gains), or a **particle filter** for the genuinely multimodal range-only posterior. **Optimize the realized information of the chosen filter, not the ideal STLOG.**

**Layer 4 (optional, the speed/scaling play) — Learn the metric or the policy.** Learn $\hat{\mathbf W}_\theta(\mathbf x,\mathbf u)$ with a physics-informed loss (Gram symmetry, frozen-$\Delta T^{2r_*+1}$ slope, $\partial f$-chain sparsity) for the 10s→100ms speedup; or learn the excitation **policy** directly with RL where the reward is the *carried filter's* realized information minus a NEES-out-of-band penalty — making safe-closure a first-class training signal that penalizes exactly the non-monotonic over-excitation the analytic metric rewarded.

**The recipe in one sentence:** *diagnose the regime, plan on the covariance, throttle to a consistency budget, whiten-and-restrict the metric to the HO subspace evaluated on the carried belief, and let a moment-matching filter actually metabolize the result* — with HO engaged only when all three §1 gauges are green and the throttle keeps the filter consistent.

## 4. Concrete next steps (ranked)

**1. Belief-space dual-control OAC on the O1 carried quad — the decisive experiment. [rt-oac; then paper.]**
- *Hypothesis:* the STLOG floor was never binding — the *EKF* was; planning on $\Sigma$ self-limits excitation to filter-supportable levels and recovers a positive HO payoff in the corner where deterministic O1 diverges.
- *Minimal experiment:* augment the carried-quad planner state with $\Sigma$, propagate the EKF Riccati with $\mathbf W^{(r)}$ as stage FIM, swap objective to $\log\det\Sigma_T$. - *Why first:* it directly tests the single most consequential claim (estimator-limited, not floor-limited) and the result decides whether closed-loop HO-OAC is rescuable in the cooperative problem at all.

**2. Port HO-OAC to the X-IO LIDAR+VIO scale/bias problem — the strongest real win. [rt-oac → paper.]**

This is the experiment that converts "HO is real in measurement-rich regimes" from assertion to demonstration.

**3. The CSTR open-loop-vs-safety-gated demonstration — the cleanest non-cooperative HO win. [paper, possibly a separate venue.]**
- *Hypothesis:* concentration/parameter estimation from temperature-only is the textbook *shallow-chain + large-$\Delta T$ + strong-nonlinearity* regime where HO observability delivers real, non-floored rank — the **mirror image** of the dead range-only corner — and OAC-shaped hot excitation measurably beats passive estimation.
- *Minimal experiment:* simulate the exothermic CSTR; compare passive UKF estimation vs HO-OAC-excited estimation on $(C_A,E)$ separation; then add a safety-MPC backstop and quantify the achievable observability gain *subject to* a runaway constraint.
- *Metric / go-no-go:* **GO** if HO excitation separates $(C_A,E)$ where passive cannot (the order-3 term breaks the one-parameter degeneracy) AND the safety-gated loop stays sub-runaway. *Value even on partial:* it cleanly demonstrates the JGCD failure was a property of the range-only *measurement/timescale regime*, not of HO observability per se — a strong framing for the paper's discussion/future-work.

**4. The adaptive-$(r,\Delta T)$ regime monitor as a reusable tool. [rt-oac.]**
- *Hypothesis:* the three online gauges ($g_{\text{rank}},g_{\text{dt}},g_{\text{info}}$) automatically reproduce every hand-found result — O0 picks $r=1$, O1-lean doesn't regress, HO engages only where it pays — with zero new divergences.
- *Minimal experiment:* wrap the existing STLOG stage cost in the adaptive shell; Hydra-sweep noise and base rate across O0-flat / O1-lean / O1-carried; check the monitor matches the best *fixed-$r$ oracle* per regime.
- *Metric / go-no-go:* **GO** if adaptive recovers O0's ~95% by auto-selecting $r=1$, doesn't regress on O1-lean, and beats fixed-low-$r$ on CRLB-vs-compute where HO pays — all with zero new divergences. This is the lowest-risk item and produces a clean figure: *the controller learning its own regime.*

**5. RL excitation policy with consistency-penalized reward — the speed/scaling bet. [rt-oac; higher-risk.]**
- *Hypothesis:* an RL policy rewarded on carried-filter realized information minus a NEES penalty learns the *monotone* part of HO excitation and the 10s→100ms speedup, on the *validated* corners (planar EKF, 3D point-mass).
- *Minimal experiment:* PPO/SAC, reward $=\Delta(-\log\det\Sigma_{\text{EKF}})-c\,\mathbb 1[\text{NEES out of band}]$; measure surrogate fidelity, wall-clock/step, bounded-rate, and a dt/noise sweep for graceful degradation.
- *Metric / go-no-go:* **GO** if learned bounded-rate $\ge$ analytic at $\ge10\times$ lower per-step cost with NEES-consistency where analytic O1 diverged. **Kill-switch (guards the documented NO-GO):** if RL recovers excitation only by re-entering the carried-quad divergence corner (cheating the reward by over-exciting), or learns only the flat dt-suppressed floor.

### Bottom line

Higher-order observability is **real but narrow, and systematically oversold** as an open-ended "more Lie derivatives" lever. Its entire benefit is *reaching a modest $r_*$* (almost always 2–3), behind a low-dimensional measurement, in fast/strongly-nonlinear dynamics observed over a horizon comparable to the buried-state timescale — and *only* when (iii) the information is physically present and (iv) closing the loop on it doesn't outrun the filter. Range-only formation flight failed three of those four conditions, which is exactly why it was a dead end and exactly why it was diagnostic. The honest forward path is **not** a better metric on the same problem; it is (a) a *measurement-rich, still-chained* port (X-IO LIDAR+VIO, scale/bias/misalignment) where conditions (i)–(iii) finally co-hold, run under (b) a *belief-space, throttled, filter-co-designed* closed loop that makes condition (iv) automatic. The single most important experiment is #1: it decides whether the carried-quad corner was estimator-limited (rescuable) or information-limited (genuinely a dead end), and that answer reframes the entire research program.

## Appendix A — Closed-loop mechanism designs (full)

### Belief-space / dual control / robust MPC
Belief-space / dual-control OAC: planning on the covariance, not the point estimate

**Why this is the right fix.** Every documented failure of the carried, full-fidelity quad comes from optimizing observability against a point estimate the filter cannot keep up with. **OBJECTIVE/METRIC DESIGN: exposing HO-only directions without lighting the divergence fuse**

**The pathology to fix.** The order-$r$ STLOG is moment-weighted: row $j$ enters with $\Delta T^{j}/j!$, so the Gram eigenspectrum spans $\Delta T^{1}\!\to\!\Delta T^{2r_*+1}$ — Hilbert-like ill-conditioning. The HO-revealed direction (the one living in $\mathcal H_{r_*-1}\setminus\mathcal H_{r_*}$, e.g. tangential velocity in O1) sits at the $\Delta T^{2r_*+1}$ floor: $\sim10^{-8}$ below the order-1 mass. Any raw aggregate metric is therefore numerically *blind* to exactly the direction HO-OAC exists to serve, and any gradient that does reach it is amplified by $1/\Delta T^{2r_*+1}$ — that amplification is precisely the **non-monotonic over-excitation**: the controller pushes infinitely hard on a near-null mode, producing estimator-destabilizing maneuvers (carried-estimate divergence).

**Why each candidate metric fails or fits.**
- **min-eig** targets the worst direction (good intent) but is non-smooth, picks up whichever mode is momentarily smallest (often a benign low-order one, not the HO target), and its subgradient through the $\Delta T^{2r_*+1}$ floor is the over-excitation driver.
- **log-det** is smooth and balances all modes, but $\log\det = \sum\log\lambda_i$ is dominated by the *well-conditioned* low-order eigenvalues; the HO direction contributes $\log(\Delta T^{2r_*+1})$ — a large-magnitude but flat term whose gradient is swamped. log-det best protects against *rank loss*, not HO exposure.
- **softmin-eig** with temperature is the right family (smooth, worst-case-leaning) but only if the spectrum is **pre-scaled**, else softmin collapses onto the $\Delta T$-floor mode and reproduces min-eig's instability.

**The design: subspace-restricted, scale-normalized softmin.**
1. **Whiten by the moment weights.** Form $\tilde{\mathbf W}=\mathbf S^{-1}\mathbf W^{(r)}\mathbf S^{-1}$, $\mathbf S=\mathrm{diag}(\Delta T^{j}/j!)$ applied to Lie-derivative *rows before* the Gram. This removes the deterministic $\Delta T$-suppression, leaving $C\propto\sigma_{\min}^2(\mathcal O^{(r_*)})$ — the *information*, not the moment — as the eigenvalue. Now the HO direction is numerically comparable, and the gradient no longer carries the $1/\Delta T^{2r_*+1}$ blow-up that drives over-excitation.
2. **Project onto the HO-only subspace.** Compute the kernel flag once at a nominal state: $\mathcal H_{r_*-1}\setminus\mathcal H_{r_*}=\mathrm{range}(P_{HO})$. Optimize $\mathrm{softmin\text{-}eig}(P_{HO}^\top\tilde{\mathbf W}P_{HO})$, i.e. the worst *HO-revealed* mode, not the global worst. This stops the metric from spending excitation on already-observable low-order modes (the regime where the **lean/rich-sensor estimator dominates** — there $P_{HO}$ is empty and the term vanishes, automatically deferring to O0).
3. **Empirical (not analytic) Gramian as the divergence guard.** Replace the truncated-series $\mathbf W^{(r)}$ with the **empirical observability Gramian** evaluated along the *carried-estimate* trajectory. The analytic STLOG measures observability about truth; the empirical one measures it about the filter's belief — closing the consistency gap that the over-excitation exploits. This is the dual-control-safe surrogate.

- **Measure:** (i) fraction of seeds with bounded estimator error (target: beat O1's 75% peak, approach O0's 95%); (ii) realized $\sigma_{\min}$ on the *HO subspace* vs. global $\lambda_{\min}$; (iii) monotonicity of bounded-fraction vs. solver effort (the over-excitation signature); (iv) effective condition number of $\tilde{\mathbf W}$ vs. raw $\mathbf W$.

WHY IT STILL DOES NOT CLEANLY SHINE (the refutation, grounded in two on-point papers).

TRAP (a) information-limit, AT THE EXCITATION POINT — fatal contradiction. The claim says to excite in the PRESLIDING regime, dithering slip reversals THROUGH v=0. But z is structurally unobservable exactly there. Saldivar et al. So the claim's headline excitation — small fast dither near v=0 in presliding — is precisely the LEAST observable regime. The HO information is concentrated at v=0 in the MODEL, but the Gramian's usable singular value lives in fast sliding. The proposed controller maximizes softmin-eig 'over the parameter+z subspace' by sweeping through zero — but the sweep spends its time where sigma_min(O^(r*))→0. That is the O0 information-limit signature transplanted to friction.

TRAP (c) richer-sensor / direct-state dominance — strong. The whole literature solves this WITHOUT HO-OAC: by an OBSERVER driven by fast-sliding PE, not by an observability-optimal dither. That joint-unidentifiability is the friction analog of rt-oac §6.12 lean-estimator dominance: a plain adaptive observer in the naturally-PE sliding phase beats an HO-OAC that has to manufacture excitation, and several buried parameters simply cannot be co-estimated from one force channel at any order.

TRAP (b) dt-suppression — only PARTIALLY escaped. So the dt^(2r*+1) floor is escaped but replaced by a noise-amplification-on-derivatives floor at the very order that carries Fc/Fs/vs. Net: not fully floored, not free.

TRAP (d) closed-loop / dual-control — the unexamined cliff. No source demonstrates that loop is stable; the structural payoff regime (presliding excitation) is also the estimator-fragile regime.

By the paper's own Thm. 1 / Prop. 5 (appendix_clean.tex lines 183-188, 350-355), once r >= r_*, lambda_min = C * dT^(2r_*+1) with the exponent FROZEN AT r_*. They claim r_* ~ 2 so the relevant weight is dT^5. Their "effective rate = speed/wavelength" argument is the right instinct, but the arithmetic is unforgiving: dT here is the STAGE horizon (delta_t << dT << T), and for a terrain-aided EKF stage at ~1.5 m/s the per-stage along-track travel is meters against terrain wavelengths of tens of meters, so the per-stage phase change they call "appreciable" is a fraction of a cycle — the curvature term v^T Hess v is multiplied by dT^5 of a sub-second-to-few-second stage and competes against single-beam echosounder noise (decimeters of clearance error over meter-scale relief). The curvature ROW being linearly independent (geometric rank, real) does NOT make it numerically present in the Gramian — this is precisely the rt-oac §6.7 lesson (orders 1->5 added geometric rank but ~nothing to lambda_min). Crucially, the WEAK-gradient/strong-curvature direction is the WORST case for dt-suppression: a strong gradient elsewhere would give a dT^3 (r_*=1) term that dominates, so the moment OAC drives toward a usable-information patch, it is driving toward a HIGHER-gradient region where r_* collapses to 1 and the curvature story evaporates. The HO benefit is self-defeating: where curvature matters (flat gradient) the info is dt-suppressed; where info is un-suppressed (steep gradient) you don't need curvature.

(c) RICHER SENSOR / BETTER FILTER (the §6.12 lean-estimator-dominance trap) — STRONGLY APPLIES. The §6.5 finding is decisive precedent: a 2nd known leader (richer h, s: 1->2) took the carried range-only loop from 25% to 90-95% bounded — the ONE thing that fixed the loop. The terrain analogue is overwhelming and standard practice: a MULTIBEAM echosounder (or a swath/sidescan) measures the terrain field across a transverse fan, sampling grad AND curvature DIRECTLY and instantaneously (s jumps from 1 to tens-hundreds of beams). That collapses r_* toward 1, raises sigma_min, AND removes the dt-suppression — buying the same information the curvature-Lie-derivative is straining to extract through order, but robustly and at the operating rate. Real terrain-aided AUV nav (TAN/TRN) overwhelmingly uses multibeam or DVL-aided point clouds precisely because single-beam is information-starved. So the honest engineering answer is "swath sensor + low-order filter dominates," exactly the §6.12 pattern: when you can MEASURE the would-be-buried field derivatives, HO is structurally moot.

(d) CLOSED-LOOP / CARRIED-DIVERGENCE (the O1 dual-control trap) — MODERATELY APPLIES, and the excitation design is a tell. The proposed excitation is cross-curvature S-turns / serpentine over saddle-like bathymetry — i.e. deliberately commanding the vehicle ONTO sharp curvature ridges. That is exactly the dual-control hazard: a terrain-aided EKF/particle filter linearizes h about x_hat, and the linearization error scales with |Hess(terrain)| — the SAME curvature the controller is chasing. Steering the carried estimate toward high-Laplacian saddles maximizes the very second-order term that breaks the filter's local-linearity assumption, inviting particle depletion / EKF inconsistency (the carried-estimate divergence, non-monotonic-in-effort, of the O1 trap). The §6.5/§6.12 verdict — no deployable single-anchor carried loop, fixed only by a 2nd measurement — ports directly. There is a real risk that optimizing order-2 STLOG HARDER drives sharper serpentines that outrun the filter, the same dual-control instability documented for O1.

NET: conditions (i)-geometric and (iii)-informational are honestly met over rough terrain (this is NOT range-only; the field is genuinely 2-D and grad is real), which is why this is the best field case and earns PARTIAL not NO. But condition (ii)-analytic (dt-suppression) is fragile-to-failing for the SPECIFIC weak-gradient/curvature direction they stake the claim on at AUV rates, and condition (iv)-safe-closure is actively endangered by the curvature-seeking excitation. Decisively, the same information is bought more robustly by motion/horizon or a swath sensor (the §6.5 2nd-anchor precedent), so HO is rarely the lever you'd actually reach for. It SHINES only in a narrow corner: single-beam-only (no multibeam available), genuinely rough/short-wavelength terrain, a long enough stage horizon to lift dT^5 off the floor, and a curvature-robust estimator (particle filter, not EKF) that survives the serpentine excitation. Outside that corner it degrades to dt-suppressed or sensor-dominated.
- **4.5/10** · partial · **Joint flux + magnetic-saturation/inductance estimation via cross-saturation curvature (deep parametric-nonlinear chain)** _(Sensorless electromechanical)_

These curvature coordinates are invisible to the first Lie derivative and surface only when the probing samples the flux map's curvature -- a genuine high-order (k>=2) appearance: v in H_1\H_2 for the saturation-parameter direction.
  - verdict: Best-constructed of the adversarial cases: it honestly clears the dt-suppression trap (fast electrical dynamics, DeltaT/tau ~ O(1), order-2/3 weight ~1e-3..1e-1, NOT the 1e-8 of rt-oac 6.7), and it concedes the headline state theta is order-1 saliency (r_*=1, HO redundant for theta). But the genuine k>=2 payoff lives ONLY in the secondary saturation-curvature parameter, and three things demote it from "shines": (1) the burial is mis-attributed. A constant saturation parameter has dot(param)=0, so it is in the kernel of every drift Lie derivative L_f^k h; it does NOT surface through the Prop-6 drift-order chain (which counts L_f steps propagating differentials backward along f). It surfaces through how the INJECTED excitation samples the curvature of the static map psi(i,theta) — an L_g / persistency-of-excitation / amplitude condition relabeled as Lie order. The order is doing less work than the injection amplitude ("you must traverse curvature to see curvature" IS a PE statement). (2) Information-fragile: Fisher info for the curvature parameter ~ (d2psi/di2)^2 * i_inj^4; physical IPMSM curvature is a small fraction of the incremental inductance, so C ∝ σ_min^2(O^(r_*)) is intrinsically small and bought by amplitude^4, not by order — slides toward information-limit if amplitude is kept small. (3) Lean-estimator/richer-sensor dominance (the 6.12 trap): cross-saturation L_dq(i_d,i_q) is routinely identified by a one-time flux-map LUT characterization or an online recursive-LS / i_d-bias-sweep regression that directly SAMPLES the map — the direct-measurement analog of IMU/AHRS collapsing the quad's r_*, making the high-order STLOG framing structurally moot. (4) Closed-loop dual-control hazard: the large-amplitude, dc-bias-sweeping curvature-seeking injection shifts the operating point and perturbs the order-1 position anchor (theta) that the curvature ID depends on — the same non-monotonic over-excitation as O1. Net: a real, non-dt-suppressed k>=2 appearance, but for the wrong (secondary) coordinate, better described as excitation design than Lie order, and dominated by direct flux-map identification.
- **4/10** · partial · **Accelerometer-bias / gravity-direction disambiguation (bias buried behind attitude under rotational excitation)** _(Visual-inertial & SLAM)_  
  - why-HO: Bias and gravity both appear as constant-ish offsets in the specific force, so they are confounded in dL_f^0..1 h. They separate only when the rotation operator R(q) modulates them differently across the horizon, i.e. when L_f^k h includes the attitude kinematics (qdot = q x omega) acting on (a - g) vs b_a. - **4/10** · partial · **Monocular feature-depth initialization via parallax-excited motion (depth at the top of the inertial chain)** _(Visual-inertial & SLAM)_  
  - why-HO: A bearing at one instant is depth-invariant (dL_f^0 h kills depth). Depth first enters through bearing-rate, which depends on translational velocity over depth (optical flow ~ v_perp/depth): an order-1 Lie derivative. With metric scale and velocity themselves buried (scenario 1), the depth-resolving rows that are METRIC (not just up-to-scale) require the inertial chain, pushing the effective r_* up. Depth is thus a genuine chained coordinate, and the controller's job is to generate parallax (translational baseline perpendicular to the bearing) that excites the order-1+ rows with metric weight.
  - verdict: The claim splits in two and HO survives in neither part as a distinct lever. (1) Up-to-scale depth via parallax is a genuine order-1 Lie derivative (bearing-rate ~ v_perp/depth), but r*=1 is exactly the regime with nothing higher to climb — it's classical triangulation, a baseline/horizon (ΔT, accumulated motion) lever, which the colleague themselves concede. Fails geometric condition (i) (needs chain depth >= 2). (2) Metric depth through the inertial chain (depth<-v<-accel<-attitude/bias) DOES push r* to ~2-3 — a real moderate chain, confirmed by the X-IO pinhole-inertial port (r*=2, rank 6->12->15: pose@0, vel+gyro-bias@1, accel-bias@2), and it is measurement-rich (2 scalars x many features), so it dodges the O0 information-limit trap (ii). That is the legitimate kernel. But it falls into two traps: (b) dt-suppression — at a 5 Hz camera stage (dt=0.2) the order-2 weight is 3.2e-4 with an order2/order1 ratio of 0.04 (~25x down), so the metric/high-order row is rank-observable but magnitude-suppressed (the exact §6.8 pattern); it only clears the floor at long horizons (dt~1.0), collapsing the benefit back onto ΔT/baseline, not order. (c) richer-sensor/lean-estimator dominance — metric scale and velocity are precisely what the IMU MEASURES (gravity-referenced strapdown); the inertial channel short-circuits the visual chain, collapsing effective r* toward 1 and making the HO bearing rows structurally moot (§6.12 lean-estimator dominance; the X-IO model gets scale from gravity in the dynamics, not from high-order bearing derivatives). It does partially ESCAPE trap (d): parallax-excitation (cross-bearing orbiting) is a classically well-conditioned, filter-safe maneuver, not the O1 carried-divergence over-excitation. Net: the high-order content is real in rank but dt-suppressed and IMU-redundant; the robust content is only order-1 parallax. So HO has no distinct role beyond order-1 triangulation + inertial scale — partial, leaning no.
